# USDT perpetual carry test

One predeclared long-spot, short-perpetual rule. At 00:05 UTC on the first day of each month, sum the preceding 30 days of already settled funding on each eligible coin. Exclude events at or after the decision. Require sufficient recent funding history. Scale that sum to the coming month's length. Enter when projected receipts exceed four-leg round-trip costs plus a 4% annual simple hurdle on the entire coin sleeve. Model equal base units with a 40% notional per leg on a separately funded 2,500 USDT sleeve for each of BTC, ETH, SOL and AVAX. Leave other collateral in cash. Use the Wave strategy's point-in-time top-three membership. Cash earns zero in the baseline. Also report against deploying the entire portfolio at an illustrative 4% per year.

Use the next completed five-minute close after the decision on each leg. On later monthly decisions trade only the change in size. Rebalance the passive control in exactly the same way but hold every eligible coin regardless of prior funding. Close all positions at period end. Compare with zero exposure, keep funded and idle coin sleeves in denominators, apply 10 bp spot and 5 bp perpetual taker fees plus 2 bp slippage per leg per transaction. Calculate signed funding on actually held quantity and settlement mark, stress by halving only positive receipts, keep all debits. Stress financing is an opportunity-cost scenario, not measured interest on USDT.

Inspect daily spot-perp basis, exact time alignment, missing prices, funding coverage and intra-period marked drawdown. Measure portfolio trade episodes from zero-to-long through flat or period end. Estimate descriptive uncertainty in total portfolio P&L using synchronized seven-day blocks of marked daily P&L, including inactive days. Gross carry, costs and break-even added transaction friction are reported independently. December 2026 remains sealed. January 2024–December 2025 is the development panel. January–September 2026 is the later historical check for this rule, subject to data availability; these market periods have been examined in other research. No parameter search.
